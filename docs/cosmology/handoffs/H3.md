## ⚠ Scope extension (added 2026-08-30 by H2 — read before anything else)

**There are TWO solver kinds to design, not one.** This prompt originally framed a single
ladder; H2's feasibility study (`docs/cosmology/observable_ladder.md` §0.1) showed O2 and
O3 are different numerical problems, distinguished by *how many oscillations a mode makes*:

| | O2 — gravitational waves | O3 — CMB photons |
|---|---|---|
| comoving `k` | `10⁻⁴–1 Mpc⁻¹` | `≈ 6.5×10²⁵ Mpc⁻¹` (`ν ~ 100 GHz`) |
| oscillations over `η₀ ≈ 1.4×10⁴ Mpc` | `~1–10³` | **`~10²⁹`** |
| tractable by stepping? | yes — the ladder below | **no integrator steps through that** |

**O3 therefore needs a different engine.** Every paper in that literature (Cembranos
arXiv:2302.08186; Domcke & Garcia-Cely arXiv:2006.01161; He et al. arXiv:2312.17636;
Kushwaha & Jain arXiv:2502.12517) removes the carrier analytically — writing the field as
`e^{−iωη} ×` a slowly varying amplitude — and integrates only the **amplitude mixing**,
a small matrix `M(η)` whose entries (`κB/2`, plasma detuning `ω_pl²/2ω`, Hubble damping
`iℋ`) sit ~30 orders of magnitude below `ω`. Because the plasma makes
`l_osc ≪ 1 pc ≪ λ_B ~ Mpc`, they then **average over coherence patches** and accumulate a
rate along the line of sight rather than resolving even the mixing oscillation.

**So design for both:**

1. **Oscillation-resolving mode-equation solver** (O2) — the ladder below, unchanged.
2. **Eikonal amplitude engine with patch averaging** (O3) — first-order `ψ' = −i M(η) ψ`
   with complex `M` (decoherence), plus a patch-averaged rate integral along the line of
   sight.

Note the reduction to the amplitude equation is an extra **symbolic** step for O3 — it must
be derived in the Wolfram pipeline (WS2), not hand-written in Python, per the project's
standing rule that no symbolic manipulation happens on the Python side. Treat that as a
constraint on the interface between WS2 and WS3, and say in your report what WS2 must
deliver for the eikonal path.

Where the two engines share machinery (batched small-matrix operations, the `M(η)`
coefficient evaluation from CAMB tables), say so — a shared core with two front-ends may
well be the right architecture, but that is your call to make and justify.

## Problem

The current modal solver evolves each Fourier mode with an exact `expm(M·t)` — valid only
for constant `M` and flat metrics ([modal.py:371-372](../../tidal/solver/modal.py) and
`:409-413` refuse otherwise). On FRW the coefficients depend on conformal time through
`a(η)`, `H(η)` (supplied numerically from CAMB tables). Required: evolve **the coupled
block** — the new sector's modes plus the standard modes they directly couple to (tensor
`h`; photon polarization) — as a small linear ODE system per `k` (~5–10 components,
maybe a few blocks), over a huge dynamic range in `η`, for O(10²–10³) k-modes, inside an
inference loop. Budget: CAMB computes full `C_ℓ` in <1 s; being 10× slower than CAMB is
acceptable, 100× is fatal. Current modal baseline: ~82 ms per full forward model.

## The escalation ladder to evaluate (from planning; validate or overturn each rung)

1. **Exponential midpoint** `expm(h·M(η+h/2))` — 2nd order, near drop-in.
2. **4th-order Gauss–Legendre Magnus** `exp(½h(M₁+M₂) − (√3/12)h²[M₁,M₂])`, batched
   across k as `(N_k, n, n)` arrays (batched matmul + batched `expm`; JAX path exists in
   `tidal/solver/modal_jax.py`). Reduces exactly to `expm(Mt)` for constant M.
   **Known limitation:** Magnus converges only for `∫‖M‖ds < π` and `‖M‖ ~ k`, so it
   still resolves every oscillation — it fixes time-dependence, not oscillation.
3. **Adiabatic/WKB regime switching** for `kη ≫ 1` — where the real speedup lives.
   **This is a research gap**: `oscode` (arXiv:1906.01421, Agocs–Handley–Lasenby–Hobson)
   and `riccati` (arXiv:2212.06924) solve scalar 2nd-order ODEs only; no off-the-shelf
   **matrix** RKWKB solver exists. Candidate matrix generalization: instantaneous
   eigenbasis for the adiabatic part + Magnus for the non-adiabatic residual — template:
   neutrino oscillations in matter (arXiv:0803.1967).
4. **Piecewise-analytic transfer matrices** — Haddadin & Handley arXiv:1809.11095
   (Mukhanov–Sasaki via repeated matrix multiplication; the group's own line of work).
5. **Emulation** (CosmoPower-class) — last resort only.

Anti-pattern to avoid: stacking N k-modes into one adaptive ODE (the error controller
forces all modes onto the worst mode's step). Batching is valid only for fixed-step
structured methods. `scikit-sundae` exposes only SUNDIALS' serial N_Vector — no k-batching
through CVODE/IDA.

## Task

1. **The integration-target decision.** Three architectures:
   (i) patch CAMB's Fortran (add our fields to its perturbation module);
   (ii) build on **DISCO-EB** (arXiv:2311.03291; github.com/ohahn/DISCO-EB) — a
   from-scratch differentiable Einstein–Boltzmann solver in JAX, per-mille agreement with
   CAMB, designed to be extended;
   (iii) **own coupled-block solver chained to unmodified CAMB** (current default
   assumption — CAMB products in, our small system evolved separately, modified
   transfer/rotation handed back).
   Evaluate on: correctness risk, per-call cost, extensibility to arbitrary
   TIDAL-derived sectors, maintenance, and how each handles the coupling of our block to
   CAMB's standard modes (one-way forcing vs two-way coupling — when is one-way valid?).
   Language is NOT a decision factor (Fortran/C/Julia all writable); architecture is.
2. **Validate/overturn the ladder** rung by rung against the literature (Magnus:
   arXiv:0810.5488; CLASS approximation-scheme lessons: arXiv:1104.2933 — the
   tight-coupling analogue question: do we need one, and what is it for our systems?).
3. **Design the matrix-WKB rung** at pseudocode level: eigenbasis tracking, non-adiabatic
   residual handling, switching criteria, failure detection (degenerate eigenvalues,
   level crossings — note our systems can have genuinely crossing frequencies).
4. **Define the benchmark protocol**: test systems (constant-M → `expm` check; de Sitter
   analytic; a stiff mixed graviton–torsion toy; a `kη ≫ 1` oscillatory case), accuracy
   metrics, timing methodology vs the ~82 ms baseline and <1 s CAMB reference.
5. **Recommend**: architecture (i/ii/iii), ladder scope for the first implementation, and
   what to defer.
